Asterisk: office PBX with PJSIP, a SIP trunk to the carrier, dial plan, queue and voicemail, NAT and a call with no audio

Asterisk 22 LTS with PJSIP for a small office: endpoints, auth and AORs for desk phones, trunk registration with the carrier, extensions.conf with internal, outbound and inbound DID calls, a queue and voicemail, NAT settings, RTP ports on the firewall, and the call where only one side can be heard.

Level 3 · Asterisk: IP PBX · 8 min read · Updated 6 Nov 2026

Rather practise it?Solve the simulated ticket "No outside calls can be made" in SysAdmin Desks ↗

What you'll get

An IP PBX for an office of 10 to 50 desks: desk phones registered over PJSIP, a SIP trunk with the carrier, a dial plan that separates internal, outbound and inbound calls, a queue for the Sales number with voicemail when nobody answers, and the NAT and firewall settings that avoid the most repeated problem in IP telephony: the call connects, but there is no audio.

Before you start

  • The version: Asterisk 22 LTS (full support until October 2028). 20 LTS now only gets security fixes. Ubuntu 24.04's package ships 20; for 22, build from source with contrib/scripts/install_prereq install, ./configure, make menuselect, make && make install && make config.
  • PJSIP is the only SIP channel: chan_sip was removed in Asterisk 21. Old guides using sip.conf no longer apply.
  • An Ubuntu Server 24.04 with a static IP (10.10.99.60) and the baseline from Ubuntu Server: users, SSH, firewall and updates.
  • From the carrier: SIP server (sip.example.com), trunk username and password, the numbers (DIDs) and the format they send them in, and their server's public IP for the firewall.
  • The office public IP (203.0.113.20) and access to the router for NAT and to turn off SIP ALG.
  • Phones in their voice VLAN, as the Cisco: VLANs and trunks guide explains.

1. The transport and NAT

It all starts in /etc/asterisk/pjsip.conf, with the transport and what Asterisk needs to know about NAT:

  • local_net: the internal networks. To talk to them Asterisk uses its private IP.
  • external_media_address and external_signaling_address: the public IP Asterisk writes into the signalling and into the SDP (the c= line that tells the other end where to send audio) when talking to anything outside local_net. Without them, the carrier receives 10.10.99.60 and sends audio to a private IP.

Changes to a transport are not applied by a reload (allow_reload defaults to no): Asterisk needs a restart, out of hours.

2. Phones: endpoint, auth and AOR

Each extension is three objects with the same name: endpoint (how it behaves), auth (username and password) and aor (where the phone is registered). Templates avoid repetition:

  • alaw is the public telephone network's codec in Europe; g722 gives wideband audio between phones that support it.
  • direct_media=no makes audio always pass through Asterisk. It costs a little more CPU, but stops two phones on different networks trying to talk directly and ending up with no audio.
  • rtp_symmetric, force_rport and rewrite_contact make Asterisk answer to the IP and port the traffic really comes from: essential for phones behind NAT (home workers).
  • Long, random passwords. An exposed PBX gets registration attempts within hours; whoever guesses an extension calls premium-rate numbers on your bill.

3. The SIP trunk with the carrier

The trunk registers with the carrier (like one more phone) and has its own endpoint. identify tells Asterisk that whatever arrives from the carrier's IP belongs to that endpoint:

Registered is what you are after. Rejected is usually username or password; Unregistered with retries means the carrier is not answering (DNS, firewall or NAT). context=entrantes is key: whatever arrives from the carrier can only do what that context allows.

4. The dial plan

/etc/asterisk/extensions.conf decides what happens to each dialled number, depending on the context the call comes from:

  • Internal: _1XX is extensions 100 to 199. No answer in 20 seconds, voicemail.
  • Outbound: dial 0 to get a line and Asterisk strips it (${EXTEN:1}). 112 goes out without a prefix. National numbers only; international and premium-rate numbers in another context that only the extensions needing it get.
  • Inbound by DID: each carrier number goes to a destination. The format (999000100, 34999000100 or +34999000100) depends on the carrier: check it on the first call with pjsip set logger on.
  • The entrantes context does not reach salientes. That separation is what stops outsiders using your PBX to call on your account.

After every change: dialplan reload in the console.

5. The queue and voicemail

  • ringall rings every phone in the queue at once; rrmemory takes turns. Queue(ventas,t,,,120) waits two minutes at most and then the call moves on to mailbox 200.
  • From a phone, *97 opens your own mailbox. To get messages by email, the server needs an MTA (Postfix relaying through your mail server).
  • queue show ventas lists the members and whether they are free, busy or unavailable.

6. Ports on the firewall and the router

  • 5060/UDP (SIP): from the voice network and from the carrier's server IP. Never open to the whole Internet.
  • 10000-20000/UDP (RTP, the audio): the default range in /etc/asterisk/rtp.conf (rtpstart and rtpend). If you narrow it, change the firewall too.

On the office router, forward the RTP range to 10.10.99.60 and turn SIP ALG off. The ALG rewrites SIP and SDP "to help" and nearly always breaks them.

7. The ticket: "on outside calls we cannot hear the customer"

Sales reports: outside calls connect, but the employee cannot hear the customer (the customer can hear them). Internal calls are fine. A fault like this is hardly ever the phone: it is NAT or the firewall.

Asterisk sends audio to the carrier and receives the phone's, but there is not a single Got from the carrier: outside audio is not getting in. In order:

  1. Which IP does Asterisk announce? With pjsip set logger on, in the SDP of the INVITE or 200 OK towards the carrier, the c=IN IP4 line. If it says 10.10.99.60, external_media_address is missing or was not applied (was Asterisk restarted after changing the transport?). If it says 203.0.113.20, move on.
  2. The router: the 10000-20000/UDP range must be forwarded to the server. A router swap or a firmware update often wipes it.
  3. SIP ALG: if the router has it on, it rewrites the SDP with other ports and the audio arrives where nobody is listening. Turn it off and restart the router.
  4. The server firewall: sudo ufw status must show the RTP range allowed.

Other endings for the same symptom:

  • Silence both ways: no codec agreement. core show channel PJSIP/... during the call shows the formats; the trunk must offer alaw.
  • The call drops after 30-32 seconds: the other end's ACK does not arrive (a Contact with a private IP, or NAT). Same family of problems: external_signaling_address, rewrite_contact and the ALG.
  • Only one home worker fails: their home router. rtp_symmetric and force_rport on their endpoint and, on the phone, a random local port and SIP ALG off on their router if possible.

After the change, a test call to a mobile in both directions and rtp set debug on showing Got and Sent for both ends. In the ticket: symptom, the c= line and RTP output before and after, and the change on the router or in the configuration.

Next steps

  • The Cisco: VLANs and trunks guide to separate voice from data with the voice VLAN.
  • Fail2ban with the Asterisk filter to block IPs trying extension passwords.
  • A daily copy of /etc/asterisk/ and /var/spool/asterisk/voicemail/: a PBX configuration is rebuilt in minutes if it is saved, and in days if it is not.
