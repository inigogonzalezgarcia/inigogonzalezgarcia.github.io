HP and Aruba switches: voice VLAN with LLDP-MED, LACP trunk, inter-VLAN routing, DHCP relay, SNMPv3 and phones that boot into the data VLAN

On ArubaOS-Switch (2530 and 2930F): the voice VLAN the phone learns over LLDP-MED, an LACP trunk to the core, the 2930F routing between VLANs with DHCP relay, SNMPv3, syslog and NTP, and IP phones that pick up an address from the user VLAN.

Level 2 · HP switches: vLANs, configuration and firmware · 8 min read · Updated 6 Nov 2026

Rather practise it?Solve the simulated ticket "The IP phones do not get the voice VLAN" in SysAdmin Desks ↗

What you'll get

Getting a floor of ArubaOS-Switch switches ready for IP phones and with its own routing: the voice VLAN the phone discovers by itself over LLDP-MED, the core link with two cables bundled by LACP, a 2930F acting as the gateway for the site's VLANs and relaying DHCP to the server, and management with SNMPv3, syslog and NTP. And solving the classic ticket of a phone system migration: the phones boot, take an address from the user network and do not register. The VLANs, backup and firmware and LLDP, loops and port-security guides cover the basics; this one starts from there.

Before you start

  • ArubaOS-Switch 16.x (show version: WC.16.xx on the 2930F, YA.16.xx or YB.16.xx on the 2530). AOS-CX has a different syntax.
  • The 2530 is Layer 2 only: it does not route between VLANs. The routing in this guide is for the 2930F (or a higher model); a 2530 only needs its management IP and its ip default-gateway.
  • VLAN 10 management (10.10.0.0/24), 20 users (10.20.0.0/24), 30 voice (10.30.0.0/24). DHCP on 10.10.0.10, with a scope for each VLAN.
  • Ports 1-44 are user ports with a phone and a PC behind it; 49 and 50 are the uplinks.

1. Voice VLAN with LLDP-MED

The phone needs to know which VLAN to tag its traffic in. With LLDP-MED, the switch tells it as soon as it is plugged in (the network policy: VLAN, priority and DSCP). For the switch to advertise it, the VLAN must be marked as voice and the port must be tagged in it:

  • voice on the VLAN is what turns on the LLDP-MED advertisement. Without it, the phone receives no VLAN and stays in the port's untagged one, the user VLAN.
  • qos dscp 46 marks voice as priority traffic (EF) on the switch.
  • lldp fast-start-count 5: when a device is plugged in, the switch sends several LLDP frames in a row instead of waiting for its 30-second interval, and the phone gets the VLAN before asking for an IP.
  • EndpointClass: Class3 and the telephone capability say the phone speaks LLDP-MED. If nothing LLDP shows on that port, the phone is not sending it (see the ticket).

2. LACP trunk to the core

In HP terms, a trunk is link aggregation (what Cisco calls EtherChannel), not the link carrying several VLANs:

  • When the ports go into trk1 they lose their VLAN configuration: VLANs are assigned to trk1. Do it from the console, or make sure your session is not going through those ports.
  • lacp negotiates with the other end; trunk 49-50 trk1 trunk (static) negotiates nothing and, with a cable in the wrong place, forms a loop. Against a Cisco, LACP on both sides (channel-group ... mode active).
  • Partner: Yes and Status: Success on both ports is correct. Partner: No means the other end is not speaking LACP.

3. Inter-VLAN routing on the 2930F

A small site may have no core: the 2930F in the comms room acts as the gateway for every VLAN.

  • With ip routing on, ip default-gateway is no longer used: the way out is the 0.0.0.0 route to the firewall (10.10.0.254), which in turn needs return routes to 10.20.0.0/24 and 10.30.0.0/24.
  • Everything the switch routes between VLANs bypasses the firewall. If users and voice must not see each other, you need an ACL on the switch or routing on the firewall.
  • The 2930F has far lower limits on routes and routed interfaces than a core switch; for a small site that is plenty.

4. DHCP relay

Each routed VLAN needs the switch to forward DHCP requests to the server:

The server picks the scope from the IP of the VLAN doing the relay (10.20.0.1 or 10.30.0.1). Each scope carries its gateway (option 3) and, in the voice scope, whatever options the phone vendor asks for (provisioning server, for example option 66).

5. SNMPv3, syslog and NTP

  • snmpv3 enable asks whether to create the initial user (initial): create it to get in, and delete it once yours exists. snmpv3 only rejects v1 and v2c even if a community is left behind.
  • operatorpriv is the read-only group with encryption; managerpriv allows writes and monitoring does not need it.
  • time timezone 60 is UTC+1 in minutes, and western-europe applies European daylight saving time.

6. The ticket: "the new phones on floor 2 do not register"

Monday, 9:15. Forty new IP phones have been deployed on floor 2. They boot, but show Not registered. In the phone's menu its IP is 10.20.0.x: the user network, not voice.

  1. Does the phone get the VLAN? show lldp info remote-device 7. If the phone appears with MED Information and telephone, it speaks LLDP-MED: the problem is what the switch advertises. If nothing appears, the phone is not sending LLDP.
  2. What does the switch advertise?

Two mistakes in floor 2's template: VLAN 30 is not marked voice and the user ports are not tagged in it. The switch advertises no voice VLAN, the phone stays in the untagged one and takes a user address.

  1. Restart the phones: a phone that already has a user address does not change it by itself. Bounce the ports (interface 1-44 disable and enable, out of hours, because it also cuts the PCs) or reboot the phones from the call platform.
  2. Check: the phone shows a 10.30.0.x address, show lldp info remote-device 7 still lists it and show mac-address 7 shows two MACs, the PC's in VLAN 20 and the phone's in 30.

If a phone has the VLAN saved by hand in its local settings (a manual VLAN setting), it ignores LLDP-MED: remove it on the phone or in the provisioning profile.

In the ticket: affected ports, what the switch was advertising and what was missing, the template change, how the phones were restarted and the voice IP of one of them as proof.
