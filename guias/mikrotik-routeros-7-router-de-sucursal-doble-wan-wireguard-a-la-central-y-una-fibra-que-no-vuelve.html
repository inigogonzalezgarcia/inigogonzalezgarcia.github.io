MikroTik with RouterOS 7 as a branch router: default firewall, fibre and 4G, WireGuard to head office, alerts and a fibre that won't come back

A restaurant or branch MikroTik managed from head office: first access with WinBox and the CLI, the default firewall rules explained, fibre and 4G with recursive routes and check-gateway, a WireGuard tunnel to head office, SNMPv3 and email alerts, the weekly backup that arrives on its own and the day traffic stays on 4G after the fibre comes back.

Sector 1 · MikroTik and TP-Link - Network and WiFi for a restaurant or hotel · 9 min read · Updated 6 Nov 2026

What you'll get

A branch router managed from head office without travelling to the site: with clean admin access, a firewall you understand rule by rule, fibre backed up by 4G and back on fibre once it recovers, a WireGuard tunnel so the POS reaches head office's ERP, SNMPv3 monitoring, an email when something changes, and the configuration saved off the device every week.

Before you start

  • A MikroTik running RouterOS 7 (hEX, RB5009, L009 or similar) on the stable or long-term channel. ether1 goes to the fibre (the ISP hands out the IP over DHCP) and ether2 to a 4G router in bridge mode or on its own 192.168.8.0/24 network.
  • WinBox 4 (Windows, macOS or Linux) or SSH. At head office, another WireGuard device (another MikroTik, a firewall or a Linux box) with its public IP or name: vpn.contoso.example.
  • Example data: branch Contoso Valencia, LAN 10.60.0.0/24, head office 10.0.0.0/16, monitoring server 10.0.10.20.
  • The venue's internal network (POS VLAN, guests, etc.) is in the MikroTik and TP-Link Omada guide. Here the router is treated as a branch device.

1. First access: WinBox, user and services

With the default configuration (defconf), the router answers on 192.168.88.1 from the LAN ports. Recent units ship with a unique password on the label; older ones, admin with no password. WinBox also finds it by MAC under Neighbors, handy when nobody knows its IP.

  • The LAN is changed to 10.60.0.1/24 (and its DHCP) so it does not overlap with any other branch: with the tunnel to head office, two branches on the same network are a problem.
  • Safe Mode (Ctrl+X in the terminal or the WinBox button) before touching services or the firewall: if you lose the session, the router rolls back the changes.

2. The default firewall, rule by rule

Do not delete it: it is a good base. /ip firewall filter print shows it; the rules carry the defconf comment.

ChainRuleWhat it is for
inputaccept established, related, untrackedReplies to what the router itself asked for (DNS, NTP, the tunnel)
inputdrop invalidPackets that match no connection
inputaccept icmpPing and the ICMP messages the network needs
inputaccept dst-address=127.0.0.1Router-internal traffic (CAPsMAN)
inputdrop all not coming from LANNothing reaches the router from outside the LAN list
forwardaccept in/out ipsec policyIPsec tunnel traffic, if any
forwardfasttrack established, relatedSpeeds up accepted connections (they skip queues and rules)
forwardaccept established, related, untrackedReplies to what went out from inside
forwarddrop invalidSame as in input
forwarddrop all from WAN not DSTNATedFrom the internet only what has a port forward gets in

The key is the interface lists (/interface list member print): ether1 and ether2 in WAN, the bridge in LAN. Every new interface (the tunnel, a VLAN) has to go into the right list or the firewall treats it as unknown. Whatever you add goes before the final drop rule of each chain.

3. Fibre and 4G: recursive routes with two probes

The base is the one from the Omada guide: the fibre default route depends on an internet destination answering through the fibre. For a branch, two probes, so that one probe going down does not send everything to 4G, and a blackhole so they are never queried over 4G:

  • check-gateway=ping pings the probe every 10 seconds; after two failures in a row the route goes inactive and the next distance wins.
  • target-scope=11 forces the probe to be resolved only through routes with scope 10 or lower: the 4G route (scope 30) does not count, so with the fibre down the probe does not "answer over 4G".
  • The probes' gateway is the gateway the ISP hands out today. If the ISP changes it, this stops working (that is the ticket in step 6, with its fix).

4. WireGuard to head office

The branch starts the tunnel; head office only listens. That way nothing has to be opened on the venue's router.

  • The branch's public key (public-key from the first print) is added as a peer at head office, with allowed-address=10.60.0.0/24,10.99.0.12/32.
  • persistent-keepalive=25s keeps the tunnel open even when the venue has no traffic, and rebuilds it over whichever link is active, 4G included.
  • Putting the tunnel in the LAN list lets head office manage the router; if you want it tighter, leave the tunnel out of LAN and add an input rule for the IT network only.
  • Traffic to 10.0.0.0/16 is not masqueraded: the default masquerade rule only acts on the WAN list.

6. The ticket: "since yesterday's outage, the venue is still going out over 4G"

Tuesday, 10:00. The mobile operator's warning: the Valencia branch has used 80% of the month's data. Yesterday afternoon there was a fibre outage; the "fibre down" email arrived, but not the "fibre restored" one.

  1. The fibre is alive: ether1 has an IP and ping 203.0.113.129 interface=ether1 answers.
  2. The probes are not: the sonda-fibra-1 and sonda-fibra-2 routes still point to 203.0.113.1, the gateway from before the outage. When it came back, the ISP handed out a different IP on a different subnet. The probe is unreachable, check-gateway keeps failing and the fibre never comes back.
  3. The fix: have the DHCP client update the probes every time it gets a lease:

Within 20 seconds, fibra-1 is As again and the "fibre restored" email arrives.

  1. What stays stuck on 4G: connections opened during the outage (the POS to its cloud, the card terminal) remain in the connection table with the 4G address translation. With the fibre active they leave through it with a source that is not theirs and hang until they expire. They are cleared when the fibre returns, by adding it to the alert:

192.168.8.100 is the MikroTik's IP on the 4G router's network. Check in IP › Firewall › Connections that no connections with that reply destination remain and that the POS syncs.

In the ticket: routes before and after, the ISP's new IP and gateway, the DHCP script and the test: unplug the fibre, take a payment, plug it back and see both alerts and the fibra-1 route active.
